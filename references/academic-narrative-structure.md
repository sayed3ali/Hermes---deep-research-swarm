# Academic Narrative Report Structure

**Version**: v1.1.2+  
**Purpose**: Enforce complete academic narrative with proper paragraphs, transitions, and integrated evidence

## Critical Requirements

1. **Stop only at three major points**: Initial Engagement, Research Planning, Final Report
2. **Show clear thinking progression**: Document evolution of understanding
3. **Connect findings explicitly**: Link sources and concepts
4. **Build coherent narrative**: Unified story, not disconnected facts

## Research Ethics

These principles govern all research activity and must be reflected in the final report:

| Principle | Requirement | Where reflected |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparency** | Always disclose limitations and uncertainties | Limitations section, Knowledge Development, footnotes |
| **Balance** | Present competing viewpoints fairly | Contradictions subsection, cross-verification analysis |
| **Recency** | Prioritize recent sources unless historical context needed | Source dates in citations, explicit justification for older sources |
| **Verification** | Flag unverified claims; don't present speculation as fact | Confidence tiers, evidence strength assessment, explicit uncertainty language |
| **Scope** | Stay within requested boundaries; note when expansion needed | Executive Summary scope statement, Limitations section |
| **Intellectual honesty** | Report contradictory findings even if they complicate conclusions | Contradictions subsection, competing evidence analysis, unresolved conflicts |

### Application in practice

**Transparency example**:
> The evidence for this conclusion is moderate: three observational studies with consistent directional findings but no randomized controlled trials. The effect size may be overstated due to publication bias toward positive results.

**Balance example**:
> While McKinsey's analysis supports the digital-first culture hypothesis, Harvard Business Review's research on structured serendipity presents a competing view that deserves equal consideration. Both lines of evidence have methodological strengths and limitations.

**Recency example**:
> The 2023 studies by Chen et al. and Rodriguez (2024) provide the most current evidence. Earlier work from 2020-2021 is cited only where it establishes foundational concepts or provides longitudinal context.

**Verification example**:
> This claim should be treated as provisional. The source is a single industry white paper without peer review, and the underlying data has not been independently verified.

**Scope example**:
> This report focuses specifically on knowledge work in OECD countries. The findings may not generalize to manufacturing, agriculture, or developing economies. Expansion beyond these boundaries would require additional research.

**Intellectual honesty example**:
> The finding that remote work increases productivity by 13% for call center workers (Bloom et al., 2025) is contradicted by the finding that creative teams show a 4% decline when fully remote (Stanford, 2025). Both findings are reported here because both are methodologically sound and contextually valid, even though their coexistence complicates any simple recommendation.

## Report Structure

### Title
```
Research Report: [Topic]
```

---

### 1. Executive Summary

**Length**: 2-3 substantial paragraphs (300-500 words)

**Content**:
- Core research question and scope
- Primary findings (synthesized, not listed)
- Overall significance and confidence level
- Key implications in brief

**Style**: Standalone summary — a reader should understand the full picture without reading further. No bullet points. Full paragraphs with transitions.

**Example opening**:
> This report investigates the commercial landscape of quantum computing in 2026, tracing how the field has evolved from laboratory curiosity to enterprise adoption. Through systematic analysis of market data, technical literature, and competitive dynamics, the research reveals a market at an inflection point: while investment has surged to $12.6 billion in private funding and $3.5 billion in government support, persistent technical limitations mean that transformative applications remain years away. The findings suggest that near-term value lies in optimization and simulation rather than the revolutionary applications often promised in popular discourse.

---

### 2. Knowledge Development

**Purpose**: Trace how understanding evolved. This is the "intellectual journey" section.

**Content** (all in narrative paragraphs):
- Initial assumptions at project start
- How assumptions were challenged, refined, or confirmed
- Key turning points where new evidence shifted perspective
- How uncertainties were resolved or acknowledged as persistent
- How confidence in claims changed as sources were cross-referenced
- Challenges encountered during research

**Style**: First-person reflective academic voice is acceptable ("Initially, we assumed..."; "However, evidence from... challenged this"). Connect each phase of understanding to specific sources.

**Example**:
> At the outset of this research, the working assumption was that quantum computing had reached commercial viability in 2026, driven by headlines about IBM's $10 billion commitment and Google's error-correction milestones. This assumption was challenged almost immediately by the landscape scan, which revealed a stark bifurcation: while investment figures were undeniably robust, enterprise adoption remained concentrated in narrow optimization use cases. The turning point came with the D-Wave UK enterprise survey (June 2026), which found that 65% of large enterprises were piloting quantum solutions but 90% of these pilots focused on workforce scheduling and supply chain — applications that do not require fault-tolerant quantum computers. This evidence shifted the research question from "Is quantum computing commercially viable?" to "Where does commercial viability actually exist, and what is the trajectory toward broader application?"

### 3. Comprehensive Analysis

#### 3.1 Primary Findings and Their Implications

**Purpose**: Core findings as flowing narrative, not bullet points.

**Content**:
- Each significant discovery explored in depth
- Supporting evidence integrated naturally into prose
- Implications analyzed within broader context
- Connections to larger patterns and trends

**Style**: Substantial paragraphs (200-400 words each). Each paragraph should have:
- Topic sentence stating the finding
- Evidence from sources woven into the narrative
- Analysis of significance
- Transition to next finding

**Example**:
> The most significant finding concerns the structure of the quantum computing market itself. Rather than a unified field, quantum computing in 2026 comprises two largely separate ecosystems: the optimization ecosystem, led by D-Wave's quantum annealers and addressing logistics and scheduling problems; and the gate-based ecosystem, led by IBM, Google, and IonQ, which aims toward general-purpose quantum computation but remains years from commercial scale. This bifurcation has profound implications for how the market should be understood. Fortune Business Insights' valuation of $2.04 billion (2026) and Persistence Market Research's more conservative $698.6 million reflect not methodological disagreement but genuine uncertainty about which ecosystem will dominate and on what timeline. The optimization ecosystem has demonstrable revenue — D-Wave reports over 100 enterprise partners — while the gate-based ecosystem is still primarily funded by research grants and speculative investment. This suggests that market valuations are highly sensitive to assumptions about error-correction timelines, a point reinforced by the finding that Google and QuEra's below-threshold error-correction milestones, while scientifically significant, do not yet translate to commercially useful qubit counts.

#### 3.2 Patterns and Trends Across Research Phases

**Purpose**: Meta-patterns visible only through synthesis.

**Content**:
- Trajectory of the field
- How individual findings coalesce into larger movements
- Robust trends vs. ephemeral signals
- Synthesis across dimensions

#### 3.3 Contradictions and Competing Evidence

**Purpose**: Fair presentation of conflicts.

**Content**:
- Conflicting sources presented with context
- Potential reasons for disagreement (methodology, sample, time period)
- Evidence quality assessment on each side
- Unresolved contradictions documented transparently

**Example**:
> A significant contradiction emerged between market projections. Fortune Business Insights projects the market reaching $18.33 billion by 2034 (31.6% CAGR), while Persistence Market Research projects $1.72 billion by 2033 (13.7% CAGR). This 10x difference in projections reflects fundamentally different assumptions about error-correction timelines. Fortune's projection assumes fault-tolerant systems will be available by 2030, enabling drug discovery and financial modeling applications. Persistence's more conservative projection assumes that optimization remains the primary commercial application through 2033. The quality of evidence favors Persistence's caution: while IBM has announced the Starling system for 2029, industry observers including McKinsey estimate that hundreds of thousands of logical qubits are needed for transformative applications, and current systems have achieved fewer than 100. This contradiction remains unresolved and represents a critical uncertainty in the market.

#### 3.4 Strength of Evidence for Major Conclusions

**Purpose**: Evaluate confidence in each conclusion.

**Content**:
- Quantity and quality of supporting sources
- Consistency across sources
- Limitations in available evidence

#### 3.5 Limitations and Gaps in Current Knowledge

**Purpose**: Acknowledge what remains unknown.

**Content**:
- Weaknesses in available evidence
- Areas where research is preliminary
- Questions that emerged but remain unanswered

#### 3.6 Integration of Findings Across Themes

**Purpose**: Show how themes reinforce each other.

**Content**:
- Connections between themes
- Unified understanding from synthesis
- Systemic insights from cross-theme analysis

---

### 4. Practical Implications

#### 4.1 Immediate Practical Applications

**Content**: Concrete, actionable recommendations. Specific guidance for practitioners.

#### 4.2 Long-Term Implications and Developments

**Content**: How findings may shape the field. Emerging trends, potential paradigm shifts.

#### 4.3 Risk Factors and Mitigation Strategies

**Content**: Risks identified, evidence-based mitigation approaches.

#### 4.4 Implementation Considerations

**Content**: Resource requirements, timelines, prerequisites, barriers.

#### 4.5 Future Research Directions

**Content**: Unanswered questions, methodological improvements, promising avenues.

#### 4.6 Broader Impacts and Considerations

**Content**: Societal, ethical, systemic implications. Connections to other fields. Unintended consequences.

---

### 5. References

**Format**: Cite Them Right Harvard (12th Edition)
- Alphabetical order by first author's surname
- Hanging indent: 0.33"
- 6pt space after each entry
- Full URLs for online sources with Accessed date

---

### 6. Appendices (if needed)

#### Appendix A: Search Strategy
- Search queries per theme
- Databases and sources consulted
- Dates of search

#### Appendix B: Source Reliability Assessment
- Evaluation criteria
- Ratings for major references

#### Appendix C: Excluded Sources
- Sources reviewed but not cited
- Reasons for exclusion

#### Appendix D: Research Timeline
- Chronology of investigation
- Key milestones

## Writing Rules

### Paragraph Structure
- **Minimum 3 sentences per paragraph**
- **Maximum 8 sentences per paragraph**
- Each paragraph must have: topic sentence, evidence, analysis, transition

### Transitions Required
- Between every section and subsection
- Between paragraphs within subsections
- Use transitional phrases: "This finding connects to...", "In contrast...", "Building on this...", "However, evidence from... challenges..."

### Evidence Integration
- Never list sources. Weave them into sentences.
- **Good**: "McKinsey's Quantum Technology Monitor (2026) found that private investment surged to $12.6 billion, a figure that contextualizes the market optimism but also raises questions about sustainability."
- **Bad**: "Source 1: McKinsey says $12.6B. Source 2: Fortune says $2.04B market."

### Prohibited
- Bullet points in body text (except Appendix A lists)
- Numbered lists in analysis sections
- Tables without narrative introduction and interpretation
- Section headings without explanatory paragraphs
- Isolated facts without context or connection

### Required
- Every section must open with an orienting paragraph
- Every subsection must explain its purpose before presenting findings
- Every finding must connect to at least one other finding
- The report must read as a single coherent argument from start to finish
